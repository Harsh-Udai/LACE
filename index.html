TL;DRText-only hard negatives leave the image encoder without near-misses. LACE swaps, removes or transplants concepts directly in the embedding to make counterfactual negatives on both sides. No diffusion, no image editor.

The problem

Same objects, same colours, different scene

a red suitcase next to a black backpack

≠

a black suitcase next to a red backpack

CLIP-style models often score these two captions almost the same, because their training never forces them to bind each attribute to the right object.

The common fix adds hard negatives on the text side only: swapped, negated, or perturbed captions. The text encoder learns the fine distinctions, but the image encoder never sees a matching near-miss. The shared embedding space becomes lopsided, even when retrieval scores look healthy.

Hubs

A few points become the nearest neighbour of almost everything and crowd out correct matches.

With LACE: hubness falls

One-way neighbours

An image's nearest caption often has a different image as its own nearest match.

Mutual reciprocity 46.57% → 51.41%

Brittle to small edits

Swapping an attribute or relation can leave the edited caption scoring above the true one.

Attribute swaps 4.65% → 1.60% · relation swaps 3.27% → 1.10%
Method

Hard negatives for both modalities, made in embedding space

LACE edits embeddings instead of pixels. It needs no diffusion model, no image editor, and no retrieval mining, so image-side counterfactuals become as cheap as text-side ones.

STEP 1 · DECOMPOSE

Find the concepts in play

Sparse coding writes each embedding as a few active atoms, such as sheep, grass, white, on. Only atoms that are both strongly used and semantically aligned are allowed to be edited.

sheep .42grass .24white .16
STEP 2 · EDIT

Change one thing

Attenuate, swap, or transplant a single concept with another of the same type: sheep → zebra, red → blue. The rest of the scene stays as it was.

sheep→zebra
STEP 3 · CONTRAST

Train both sides

Edited captions become negatives for image→text retrieval, and edited images become negatives for text→image retrieval. At test time all of this is gone: it is a plain dual encoder.

image → texttext → image

Do the edits mean anything?

Edited embeddings retrieve the new concept

We transplant a concept inside a real COCO image embedding and look up its nearest real images.

  1. Before the editNeighbours show the source concept: sheep in one row, donuts in the other.
  2. After a transplantNeighbours switch to the target. Sheep → zebra brings in two zebras; donut → broccoli turns all three into broccoli. Grass, plates and tables stay.
  3. A random edit of the same sizeNever retrieves the target. The direction of the edit carries the meaning, not its size.

Why it's practical

What LACE doesn't need

Diffusion model

No generated images. Counterfactuals are made in embedding space.

Image editor

No inpainting or pixel edits to make image-side negatives.

Retrieval mining

No search over a corpus for near-miss pairs.

At test time, it is a plain dual encoder.The same generator works with CLIP's contrastive loss and SigLIP's pairwise sigmoid loss.
